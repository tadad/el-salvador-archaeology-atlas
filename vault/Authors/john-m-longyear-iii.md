# John M. Longyear III

**John Munro Longyear III** (July 30, 1914–March 27, 2011) was an American archaeologist and anthropology professor. He earned degrees from Cornell University and Harvard University, conducted archaeological fieldwork in Central America and the United States, and taught at Colgate University for about thirty years. He established Colgate's anthropology museum in the 1950s; it was renamed the Longyear Museum of Anthropology when he retired in 1978.

## Contributions

Longyear directed an Institute for Andean Research expedition to El Salvador from September 1941 through April 1942. He concentrated on archaeological reconnaissance east of the Río Lempa and excavated Los Llanitos near San Miguel. His collaborator [[stanley-h-boggs|Stanley H. Boggs]] surveyed much of central and western El Salvador and contributed to the resulting report. Longyear published the expedition as [[Papers/longyear|Archaeological Investigations in El Salvador]] in 1944 and later produced important studies of Copán ceramics and the southeastern Maya frontier.

## Longyear family and name disambiguation

Longyear was the third generation in his family named John Munro Longyear:

- His grandfather, **John Munro Longyear Sr.** (1850–1922), was a Michigan timber and mining magnate. He established the Arctic Coal Company settlement on Svalbard that became Longyearbyen. Archaeological citations do not refer to him.
- His father, **John Munro Longyear Jr.** (born 1889), worked in mining and geology and managed family mining interests.
- The archaeologist was **John Munro Longyear III** (1914–2011). Citations such as “John M. Longyear,” “J. M. Longyear III,” and “Longyear 1944” normally refer to him.

Their earlier ancestor **John Wesley Longyear** (1820–1875), the archaeologist's great-grandfather, was a U.S. representative and federal judge. Although he shared the first name John, his full name was not John Munro Longyear.
