# Tecpa, Zapotitán

## Description

A reported Indigenous and colonial-period settlement in the Zapotitán basin, known in early sources as Tecpa or San Juan Tecpán. This record preserves a research lead and is intentionally excluded from the atlas because the ingested sources do not establish a reproducible location or boundary.

## Studies

### 2010: Retrospective report of a looting inspection

Valdivieso recalled that government archaeologists inspected active looting at a place called Tecpan in La Libertad and recovered material, but he supplied neither the date of the incident nor a coordinate, map, artifact inventory, or site boundary. The account indicates that a Tecpan locality was recognized administratively by this period without resolving whether it is Lardé's settlement. [[Papers/2010-valdivieso-remembranzas-arqueologia#Page 17|Source: 2010-valdivieso-remembranzas-arqueologia.pdf, PDF p. 17]]

### 1943: Identification caution

Boggs recorded a local proposal that San Andrés was actually Tecpán, but explicitly stated that the colonial-period city had not been identified. He considered the same documentary reference potentially applicable to San Andrés, Sitio del Niño, Hacienda Zapotitán, or Hacienda Presa. [[Papers/tzunpame_boggs_sanandres#Page 16|Source: tzunpame_boggs_sanandres.pdf, PDF p. 16]]

### 1924: Geological-historical synthesis

Lardé briefly placed Tecpa west of Sitio del Niño and argued that changes to Río Sucio inundated the settlement and the Zapotitán basin. [[Papers/larde-1924-geologia-centro-america#Page 20|Source: PDF p. 20]]

### 1923: Archival location argument and reported mounds

Using seventeenth-century titles for Las Lajas and Zapotitán, Lardé reconstructed Tecpa between Río La Joya, Río Sucio, and Laguna de Zapotitán. He reported that near a locality then called Ticpa he saw many mounds, some described as funerary, but published no map, coordinate, excavation, artifact description, or chronology. This observation requires archival and field verification. [[Papers/larde-1923-volcan-izalco#Page 159|Source: PDF pp. 159–165 (printed pp. 70–73)]]

### 1914: Earlier location hypothesis

Barberena identified Tecpa with San Juan Tecpán and placed it in or near Hacienda Las Lajas. Lardé later disputed this location using the estate titles. [[Papers/historiadeelsalv01barb#Page 326|Source: PDF pp. 326–327 (printed pp. 310–311)]]
