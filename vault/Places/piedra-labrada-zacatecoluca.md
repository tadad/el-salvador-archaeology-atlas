# Piedra Labrada, Zacatecoluca

## Description

The site is a boulder measuring approximately 3.8 by 4.18 metres, with most of its carvings on a flat, slightly inclined upper surface. The clearest ancient motif is a serpent head near the center of the rock, facing north. Coladán and Amaroli assign it stylistically to the Postclassic. A second carving with similar grooves lies at the north end, but its subject is unidentified. [Coladán and Amaroli, “Las representaciones rupestres de El Salvador,” PDF p. 164 (printed p. 167)](https://siarb-bolivia.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/americacentral.pdf)

## Later markings

Crosses, personal names, and the word *botija* were cut into the rock after the ancient motifs. The authors connect *botija* with local stories about clay vessels containing buried coins. These later marks are graffiti, not part of the Postclassic serpent design.

## Location and uncertainty

The published description places the rock at Plan de la Nancera, Hacienda La Fortaleza, on the coastal plain near Zacatecoluca, but gives no coordinates. The marker is therefore a deliberately broad Zacatecoluca-vicinity proxy, not the rock's location. The national fieldwork synthesis independently lists Piedra Labrada among the places addressed by the 1998 rock-art project.
