# Barrio Concepción buried horizon

## Description

A reported findspot in northeastern San Salvador where a well on property belonging to a Mr. Mac Intire exposed Indigenous pottery about five or six varas deep, in dark soil beneath a thick white volcanic-ash deposit. The report does not preserve the parcel address, and the artifacts were not recovered through controlled excavation. It is one locality within the broader buried occupation horizon described by Lardé, not evidence for a separately bounded city. [[Papers/arqueologia-cuzcatleca-valle-de-san-salvador#Page 5|Source: PDF pp. 5–6]]

## Studies

### 1924: Published stratigraphic interpretation

Lardé used the reported depth and position below the white ash as an initial clue that archaeological material rested on an older land surface. His original culture label and chronology are obsolete; the reproducible observation is the reported stratigraphic relationship. [[Papers/arqueologia-cuzcatleca-valle-de-san-salvador#Page 5|Source: arqueologia-cuzcatleca-valle-de-san-salvador.pdf, PDF pp. 5–6]]

### 1917: Accidental discovery

According to information relayed to Lardé by Carlos Renson, pottery was encountered while Mac Intire opened a well in Barrio Concepción. The work was not an archaeological excavation. [[Papers/arqueologia-cuzcatleca-valle-de-san-salvador#Page 5|Source: arqueologia-cuzcatleca-valle-de-san-salvador.pdf, PDF p. 5]]
