# Joya de Cerén

## Overview

A Middle/Late Classic village buried by a volcanic eruption, where excavations exposed structures and cultivated fields.

## Chronology

The park history dates the burial to about 640 CE. GPR was collected in 1979, attempted again in the wet season of 1992, and expanded digitally in 1994; excavation began in 1989.

## How it was investigated

Investigators collected more than 7.8 km of GPR profiles across five grids, converted travel time to depth, and modeled the buried living surface. The 1979 and 1994 dry-season datasets were productive; wet ground attenuated the 1992 survey.

## What archaeologists found

GPR recognized eight of nine excavated structures within the survey grids and 18 additional structure-like anomalies awaiting confirmation. It also mapped a buried valley, drainage, plazas, household clusters, fields, and possible clay-quarrying areas.

## Research history

### 2014 — Reinterpretation

A site study reassessed identity, agency, representation, and site-management narratives.

**Evidence:** [[Papers/anales-53#Page 236|anales-53.pdf, PDF pp. 236–257]]

### 2013 — Collection analysis

A thesis analyzed Copador vessel contexts, forms, and chronology.

**Evidence:** [[Papers/anales-54#Page 10|anales-54.pdf, PDF pp. 10–39]]

### 1989–2011 — Fieldwork

Repeated excavation seasons investigated architecture, households, daily life, and crops; the project was ongoing when reported at the 2011 symposium.

**Evidence:** [[Papers/2012-escamilla-fowler-practica-arqueologia#Page 5|2012-escamilla-fowler-practica-arqueologia.pdf, PDF pp. 5, 9 (printed pp. 431, 435)]]

### 1994 — Fieldwork

A digital GPR campaign added 3,800 m of 300 and 500 MHz profiles; combined with digitized 1979 data, five grids covered about 2 ha.

**Evidence:** [[Papers/1995-conyers-gpr-ceren#Page 8|1995-conyers-gpr-ceren.pdf, PDF pp. 8–9]]

### August 1992 — Fieldwork

A rainy-season GPR attempt suffered signal attenuation at 2–3 m and identified no significant anomalies.

**Evidence:** [[Papers/1995-conyers-gpr-ceren#Page 7|1995-conyers-gpr-ceren.pdf, PDF p. 7]]

### 1979–1980 — Fieldwork

The initial project conducted several geophysical investigations, including magnetic-resistance and radar methods.

**Evidence:** [[Papers/2012-escamilla-fowler-practica-arqueologia#Page 5|2012-escamilla-fowler-practica-arqueologia.pdf, PDF p. 5 (printed p. 431)]]

### June 1979 — Fieldwork

A dry-season 80 MHz GPR survey collected roughly 4,000 m of profiles over about 1 ha; drilling later confirmed one structure-like anomaly.

**Evidence:** [[Papers/1995-conyers-gpr-ceren#Page 6|1995-conyers-gpr-ceren.pdf, PDF pp. 6–8]]

## Location and uncertainty

GPR surveys in 1979, 1992, and 1994 preceded and accompanied the excavation program; dry-season surveys in 1979 and 1994 produced the useful mapping results.

## Sources

### 1995-conyers-gpr-ceren.pdf

**Citation:** PDF pp. 1–2, study summary and buried-site inventory

[[Papers/1995-conyers-gpr-ceren#Page 1|Open vault paper]] · [Open cited source](https://antharky.ucalgary.ca/caadb/sites/antharky.ucalgary.ca.caadb/files/Conyers_1995_The_Use_of_Ground-Penetrating_Radar_To_Map_the_Buried_Structures_and_Landscape_of_the_Ceren_Site.pdf#page=1)

Conyers analyzed more than 7,800 m of GPR profiles to map the buried living surface and structures through as much as 5–6 m of volcanic overburden. The paper reports 26 structures identifiable on profiles, including excavated examples used for comparison and additional anomalies not yet confirmed by drilling or excavation.

### 1995-conyers-gpr-ceren.pdf

**Citation:** PDF pp. 7–9, 1979, 1992, and 1994 survey history and acquisition

[[Papers/1995-conyers-gpr-ceren#Page 7|Open vault paper]] · [Open cited source](https://antharky.ucalgary.ca/caadb/sites/antharky.ucalgary.ca.caadb/files/Conyers_1995_The_Use_of_Ground-Penetrating_Radar_To_Map_the_Buried_Structures_and_Landscape_of_the_Ceren_Site.pdf#page=7)

The 1979 dry-season survey collected about 4,000 m of 80 MHz data over roughly 1 ha; an anomaly was later confirmed as a buried structure. An August 1992 attempt was limited by rainy-season moisture and found no significant anomalies. In 1994, 3,800 m of digital 300 and 500 MHz data were added, bringing the five-grid coverage to about 2 ha.

### 1995-conyers-gpr-ceren.pdf

**Citation:** PDF pp. 18–23, structure identification and paleolandscape interpretation

[[Papers/1995-conyers-gpr-ceren#Page 18|Open vault paper]] · [Open cited source](https://antharky.ucalgary.ca/caadb/sites/antharky.ucalgary.ca.caadb/files/Conyers_1995_The_Use_of_Ground-Penetrating_Radar_To_Map_the_Buried_Structures_and_Landscape_of_the_Ceren_Site.pdf#page=18)

Eight of nine excavated or partly excavated structures inside the grids were recognizable in the radar data, and 18 additional structure-like anomalies awaited confirmation. Depth-corrected profiles reconstructed a small valley, drainage channels, bluffs, plazas, household clusters, fields, and likely quarrying areas. These are geophysical interpretations, not excavated inventories of every anomaly.
