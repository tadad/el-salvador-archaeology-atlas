# El Chaparral

## Overview

An excavated locality on a steep part of the Torola River's south bank. It has retaining walls on three terraces, but their exact date is unknown.

## Chronology

The walls postdate Late Classic material beneath them and may be colonial. Excavation ran from January 16 to February 15, 2013.

## How it was investigated

Twelve test pits, mostly 2 × 2 metres, sampled the roughly 100 × 50 metre locality and its three terraces.

## What archaeologists found

Excavators documented poorly preserved walls and a semicircular feature. Ceramics, chipped stone, metates, and grinding stones were mostly in secondary deposits and did not establish the walls' date.

## Research history

### 2016 — Collection analysis

A projectile-point thesis analyzed three artifacts from El Chaparral.

**Evidence:** [[Papers/2016-barrera-puntas-flecha-oriente#Page 54|Análisis morfológico y funcional de las puntas de flecha del oriente de El Salvador, PDF pp. 54–56, 79–91]]

### 16 January–15 February 2013 — Fieldwork

A rescue excavation opened 12 test pits.

**Evidence:** [[Papers/anales-54#Page 61|anales-54.pdf, PDF pp. 61, 65–67]]

### 2008–2009 — Site recording

The systematic regional survey discovered and recorded the site.

**Evidence:** [[Papers/anales-54#Page 61|anales-54.pdf, PDF pp. 61, 65–67]]

## Sources

### Análisis morfológico y funcional de las puntas de flecha del oriente de El Salvador

**Citation:** PDF pp. 54–56, 79–91, site summary and three-point analysis

[[Papers/2016-barrera-puntas-flecha-oriente#Page 54|Open vault paper]] · [Open cited source](https://biblioteca.utec.edu.sv/siab/virtual/tesis/941000733.pdf#page=54)

The thesis analyzes three superficially recovered projectile points associated with the structural sector and assigns the studied material to the Late Classic. This supports a broad Classic link for the archaeological material, but it does not date the terrace walls themselves, which the excavation report treats as later and uncertain.

### anales-54.pdf

**Citation:** PDF p. 61 (printed p. 61), abstract and site setting

[[Papers/anales-54#Page 61|Open vault paper]] · [Open cited source](https://www.cultura.gob.sv/download/revista-anales-54/?wpdmdl=3364#page=61)

The report places the site on a steep part of the Torola's south bank in the hydroelectric-project impact area, documents its discovery during 2009 survey, and gives a minimum area of 5,000 m². It calls the terrace walls probably agricultural but chronologically uncertain; Late Classic material on the surface prompted excavation.

### anales-54.pdf

**Citation:** PDF pp. 65–67 (printed pp. 65–67), project history, Figure 1, and field methods

[[Papers/anales-54#Page 65|Open vault paper]] · [Open cited source](https://www.cultura.gob.sv/download/revista-anales-54/?wpdmdl=3364#page=65)

The site was registered as PACH-01-08 and measured about 100 × 50 m. Fieldwork ran from January 16 to February 15, 2013, and opened 12 test pits, mostly 2 × 2 m, across three terraces. Figure 1 is an internal topographic plan with no published coordinate or geodetic grid, so it supports only an approximate marker.

### anales-54.pdf

**Citation:** PDF p. 69 (printed p. 69), excavation results and photographs 1–2

[[Papers/anales-54#Page 69|Open vault paper]] · [Open cited source](https://www.cultura.gob.sv/download/revista-anales-54/?wpdmdl=3364#page=69)

The results describe poorly preserved construction: three walls and a semicircular feature on the upper terrace, two walls on the middle terrace, and one wall on the lower terrace. The limited associated stratigraphy and material prevented an exact chronology.

### anales-54.pdf

**Citation:** PDF pp. 75–76 (printed pp. 75–76), material study and photographs 3–4

[[Papers/anales-54#Page 75|Open vault paper]] · [Open cited source](https://www.cultura.gob.sv/download/revista-anales-54/?wpdmdl=3364#page=75)

The study inventoried 932 ceramic fragments, 120 lithic pieces, metates, grinding stones, charcoal samples, and a horseshoe. The ceramic material was sparse, eroded, and in secondary deposits; diagnostic Early and Late Classic sherds therefore do not provide a secure date for the walls.

### anales-54.pdf

**Citation:** PDF pp. 78–79 (printed pp. 78–79), interpretation and conclusions

[[Papers/anales-54#Page 78|Open vault paper]] · [Open cited source](https://www.cultura.gob.sv/download/revista-anales-54/?wpdmdl=3364#page=78)

The authors conclude that none of the ceramic or lithic material was in primary position and that the walls can be dated only after the Late Classic. They suggest, without establishing, a colonial date and agricultural function.
