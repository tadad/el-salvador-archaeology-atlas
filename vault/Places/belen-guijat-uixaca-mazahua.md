# Belén-Güijat / Uixaca-Mazahua

## Description

A historically reported Indigenous and colonial settlement locality near Belén-Güijat and El Desagüe at the outlet of Lake Güija. Lardé equated ruins in this area with both Güijar and Mazahua-Uixaca; Longyear repeated the proposal. Later historical research finds the relevant settlement names inconsistently represented in the documents and cautions against treating them as one securely identified place. A recent report of dense Copador Polychrome ceramics applies only to the broader Belén-Güijat zone and does not locate the historical settlement. This record therefore preserves the research problem without placing a speculative atlas marker. [[Papers/longyear#Page 81|Source: PDF p. 81]] [[Papers/2009-erquicia-sitios-arqueologicos-historicos#Page 8|Source: PDF pp. 8–9]] [[Papers/2026-flores-peregrinaje-lempa-guija#Page 33|Source: PDF p. 33]]

## Studies

### 2025: Reported ceramic observation

A 2026 synthesis reports, from a 2025 personal communication with Paul Amaroli, that the Belén-Güijat zone contains an unusually dense deposit of Copador Polychrome sherds. No coordinate, collection method, assemblage table, or demonstrated connection to Uixaca-Mazahua is supplied. [[Papers/2026-flores-peregrinaje-lempa-guija#Page 33|Source: 2026-flores-peregrinaje-lempa-guija.pdf, PDF p. 33]]

### 2009: Historical reassessment

Erquicia and Herrera reviewed colonial and later accounts of abandoned communities around Lake Güija. They emphasized imprecise citations and unresolved alternatives, and noted that investigators had not verified the reported submerged buildings. Their documented field coordinate belongs to the separate [[Places/ostua|Ostúa church ruins]], not to Belén-Güijat. [[Papers/2009-erquicia-sitios-arqueologicos-historicos#Page 8|Source: 2009-erquicia-sitios-arqueologicos-historicos.pdf, PDF pp. 8–9]]

### 1944: Published site list

Longyear's appended inventory placed Belén, also called Belén-Güijat and Tablón de Belén, south of and near the outlet of Lake Güija. It repeated Lardé's identification of stone remains in the region with Güijar and Uixaca-Mazahua but did not document a survey coordinate. [[Papers/longyear#Page 81|Source: longyear.pdf, PDF p. 81]]

### 1924: Historical-geological identification

Lardé rejected the story that the settlement lay submerged beneath Lake Güija and instead placed the reported ruins near the outlet by Belén-Güijat. His equation with colonial-period Mazahua-Uixaca remains an unverified historical interpretation. [[Papers/larde-1924-geologia-centro-america#Page 11|Source: larde-1924-geologia-centro-america.pdf, PDF pp. 11–12]]
