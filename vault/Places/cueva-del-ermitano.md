# Cueva del Ermitaño

## Description

Cueva del Ermitaño is a double rock shelter rather than a deep geological cave. The upper shelter contains three red negative handprints. Two appear together, one below a green stepped-fret frame enclosing an unidentified animal-like figure with an undulating body; a third includes the forearm. A dark red or purple sign also survives in this shelter. The lower shelter has a line of approximately ten red negative handprints at mid-height. Nearby shelters inspected to the west had no paintings or engravings. [Coladán and Amaroli, “Las representaciones rupestres de El Salvador,” PDF p. 163 (printed p. 166)](https://siarb-bolivia.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/americacentral.pdf)

A white inscription reading “Huezo Cordoba, 1965, Museo Nacional” is a modern marking, not an ancient painting.

## Dating and context

A later regional review reports Escamilla's finding that neither Cueva del Ermitaño nor El Tablón yielded ceramic or lithic material, making their dating difficult. A proposed Late Classic or Early Postclassic date for Ermitaño is based on stylistic comparison and remains a hypothesis. [Perrot-Minnot, Costa, and Manzano, *La Cueva del León, Chalatenango: Arte rupestre*, PDF pp. 8–9](https://repositorio.ues.edu.sv/bitstreams/7a19a959-6ac8-4455-b01a-e64920f07c64/download)

## Location and uncertainty

Locality sources place Cueva del Ermitaño in the El Manzano sector of Cantón El Ocotal, about 9 km from Dulce Nombre de María. A university study gives coordinates for Parque Ecológico El Manzano, but not for the shelter. The atlas therefore uses El Manzano as a generalized proxy. [University of El Salvador study, El Manzano locality and coordinates](https://repositorio.ues.edu.sv/server/api/core/bitstreams/02260d20-139d-4bba-85c6-9359412c66b7/content)
