# Casa Blanca

## Overview

An excavated mound group at the northeastern edge of Chalchuapa, part of which is protected within Casa Blanca Archaeological Park.

## Chronology

Late Preclassic, Early Classic, and Postclassic components are reported.

## How it was investigated

Longyear made a cursory inspection in 1942 and reported that two mounds had already been partly excavated. A 1995–2000 project excavated, mapped, and restored some structures.

## What archaeologists found

Longyear counted sixteen mounds, described adobe-and-stone construction, and recorded ceramics, vessels with burned human bones, and stone sculptures. The 1995–2000 project reported a burial with offerings and three Plumbate vessels associated with Structure 2.

## Research history

### 1995–2015 — Archival synthesis

A twenty-year program of research, conservation, publication, and public archaeology was reviewed.

**Evidence:** [[Papers/anales-56#Page 127|anales-56.pdf, PDF pp. 127–135]]

### 2008 — Fieldwork

Structure 5 and Mound 6 were investigated during restoration work.

**Evidence:** [[Papers/anales-56#Page 42|anales-56.pdf, PDF p. 42]]

### 1995–2000 — Fieldwork

The Japanese interdisciplinary project conducted excavation, restoration, mapping, and analysis.

**Evidence:** [[Papers/2007-erquicia-los-gavilanes#Page 3|2007-erquicia-los-gavilanes.pdf, PDF p. 3]]

### 1966–1970 — Fieldwork

The University of Pennsylvania Chalchuapa Project included Casa Blanca in regional and site research.

**Evidence:** [[Papers/2012-escamilla-fowler-practica-arqueologia#Page 3|2012-escamilla-fowler-practica-arqueologia.pdf, PDF p. 3 (printed p. 429)]]

### 1942 — Site recording

The locality was inspected during the Chalchuapa reconnaissance.

**Evidence:** [[Papers/longyear#Page 24|longyear.pdf, PDF p. 24 (printed p. 17)]]

## Sources

### longyear.pdf

**Citation:** PDF p. 24 (printed p. 17), ‘Casa Blanca Group’ and Figure 6

[[Papers/longyear#Page 24|Open vault paper]] · [Open cited source](https://fundar.org.sv/referencias/longyear.pdf#page=24)

Longyear places the group at the northeast corner of Chalchuapa, maps it in Figure 6, and reports his cursory 1942 inspection. He counted 16 mounds, described adobe-and-stone construction, noted that two mounds had already been partly excavated, and recorded surface ceramics, vessels with burned human bones, and stone sculptures.

### parks.pdf

**Citation:** PDF p. 2, ‘Casa Blanca’

[[Papers/parks#Page 2|Open vault paper]] · [Open cited source](https://fundar.org.sv/referencias/parks.pdf#page=2)

The park summary identifies Casa Blanca as a pyramid group on the edge of Chalchuapa, reports Late Preclassic and Early Classic occupation, and says it officially opened as an archaeological park in 2004. It does not publish a site coordinate or assign a culture.

### 2007-erquicia-los-gavilanes.pdf

**Citation:** PDF p. 3 (printed p. 855), Casa Blanca project summary

[[Papers/2007-erquicia-los-gavilanes#Page 3|Open vault paper]] · [Open cited source](https://www.asociaciontikal.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/50.06_-_Erquicia.pdf#page=3)

The summary dates the interdisciplinary project to 1995–2000 and reports Postclassic features: a burial with offerings east of Pyramid 5 and three Plumbate vessels associated with the final construction stage of Structure 2.

### 2012-escamilla-fowler-practica-arqueologia.pdf

**Citation:** PDF p. 7 (printed p. 433), Japanese project section

[[Papers/2012-escamilla-fowler-practica-arqueologia#Page 7|Open vault paper]] · [Open cited source](https://www.asociaciontikal.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/036_Escamilla_y_Fowler.pdf#page=7)

The review says the five-year project began in 1995 and conducted excavation and restoration of some park structures, multidisciplinary research, and precise mapping. It does not itself establish the site's occupation dates or a culture assignment.
