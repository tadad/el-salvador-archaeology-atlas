# Casa Blanca

## Description

An excavated mound group at the northeastern edge of Chalchuapa, part of which is protected within Casa Blanca Archaeological Park. Longyear counted sixteen mounds, described adobe-and-stone construction, and recorded ceramics, vessels with burned human bones, and stone sculptures. [[Papers/parks#Page 2|Source: PDF p. 2, ‘Casa Blanca’]] [[Papers/longyear#Page 24|Source: PDF p. 24 (printed p. 17), ‘Casa Blanca Group’ and Figure 6]]

## Studies

### 1995–2015: Archival synthesis

A twenty-year program of research, conservation, publication, and public archaeology was reviewed. [[Papers/anales-56#Page 127|Source: anales-56.pdf, PDF pp. 127–135]]

### 2008: Fieldwork

Structure 5 and Mound 6 were investigated during restoration work. [[Papers/anales-56#Page 42|Source: anales-56.pdf, PDF p. 42]]

### 1995–2000: Fieldwork

The Japanese interdisciplinary project conducted excavation, restoration, mapping, and analysis. [[Papers/2007-erquicia-los-gavilanes#Page 3|Source: 2007-erquicia-los-gavilanes.pdf, PDF p. 3]]

### 1966–1970: Fieldwork

The University of Pennsylvania Chalchuapa Project included Casa Blanca in regional and site research. [[Papers/2012-escamilla-fowler-practica-arqueologia#Page 3|Source: 2012-escamilla-fowler-practica-arqueologia.pdf, PDF p. 3 (printed p. 429)]]

### 1942: Site recording

The locality was inspected during the Chalchuapa reconnaissance. Longyear made a cursory inspection in 1942 and reported that two mounds had already been partly excavated. Longyear counted sixteen mounds, described adobe-and-stone construction, and recorded ceramics, vessels with burned human bones, and stone sculptures. [[Papers/longyear#Page 24|Source: longyear.pdf, PDF p. 24 (printed p. 17)]]
