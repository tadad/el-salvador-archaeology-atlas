# La Laguneta

## Description

A roughly ten-hectare settlement beside the Río Gaspar near the Lempa, with about 15 structures, 13 additional architectural features, an I-shaped ballcourt, plaza, and acropolis. Continuous occupation from the Late Preclassic through the Late/Terminal Classic. Although one sentence extends the sequence into the Early Postclassic, the same report says the site was abandoned before about 900 CE and its diagnostic table ends with the Lepa phase; the conservative ontology therefore stops at the Classic. The project documented about 15 structures and 13 additional features, including the ballcourt, acropolis, plaza, residential mounds, and perimeter walls. ## Studies

### 2015: Regional isotopic proxy study

Researchers could not map La Laguneta directly and instead used animal samples from Puente Titihuapa as a regional geological proxy. [[Papers/anales-54#Page 122|Source: anales-54.pdf, PDF p. 122]]

### By 2010: Prior archaeological study

A 2014 secondary account cites an earlier study by Amador and colleagues, reporting at least 15 structures, recovered materials, and a Late-to-Terminal Classic occupation. The underlying report is not mapped to a local PDF in this corpus. [[Papers/anales-53#Page 81|Source: anales-53.pdf, PDF p. 81]]

### 2006–2008: Mapping and test excavation

The Atlas team used total station, GPS, and GIS to produce a new site map, excavated seven stratigraphic test pits, exposed part of the ballcourt, collected surface material, and analyzed the ceramics; the exact field season is unstated. The project documented about 15 structures and 13 additional features, including the ballcourt, acropolis, plaza, residential mounds, and perimeter walls. It recovered 1,883 ceramic sherds and 225 lithic fragments and used the ceramics to construct a Late Preclassic–Late/Terminal Classic sequence. [[Papers/2009-amador-atlas-arqueologico-oriente#Page 45|Source: Atlas Arqueológico de la Región de Oriente de El Salvador, PDF pp. 45–48]]
