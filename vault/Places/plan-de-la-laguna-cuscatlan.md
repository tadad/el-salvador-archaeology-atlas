# Plan de la Laguna (Cuscatlán survey landscape)

## Description

The former Laguna de Cuscatlán crater and its margins anchor a broad archaeological landscape in modern Antiguo Cuscatlán. The marker represents the surveyed landscape rather than one bounded site or excavation. Surface remains around the crater were extensive and nearly continuous, though their density varied. [[Papers/2025-flores-manzano-despues-ilopango#Page 2|Source: PDF pp. 2–3, 8–10 (printed pp. 12–13, 18–20), landscape and survey synthesis]] [[Papers/buscacuscatlan#Page 89|Source: PDF pp. 89–90 (printed pp. 82–83), intensive survey methods and results]]

## Studies

### 2024–2025: Archival synthesis

Flores Manzano reexamined the survey and rescue record and proposed the former lagoon as a potentially meaningful ritual landscape; that interpretation is explicitly distinguishable from the survey observations. The distribution supports a substantial settlement landscape but did not reveal an undisputed capital core or ballcourt. [[Papers/2025-flores-manzano-despues-ilopango#Page 2|Source: 2025-flores-manzano-despues-ilopango.pdf, PDF pp. 2–3, 8–10, 35–38]]

### December 1984–February 1985: Fieldwork

An intensive pedestrian survey used parallel transects, recorded surface features, and made 49 diagnostic collections across the wider Cuscatlán study area, including extensive remains around the former lagoon. From December 1984 through February 1985, investigators walked intensive parallel transects generally 20–30 m apart, recorded visible platforms and stone concentrations, and made 49 diagnostic surface collections. Surface remains around the crater were extensive and nearly continuous, though their density varied. [[Papers/buscacuscatlan#Page 89|Source: buscacuscatlan.pdf, PDF pp. 89–90 (printed pp. 82–83)]]
