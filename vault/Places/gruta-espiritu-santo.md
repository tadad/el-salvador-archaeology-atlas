# Gruta del Espíritu Santo

## Description

Gruta del Espíritu Santo is a large rock-art shelter in an ignimbrite formation near Corinto. Its walls carry multicoloured paintings and some engravings of human figures, hands, animals, and geometric signs; the art itself remains undated, and ceramics and stone tools found at the shelter do not establish when the images were made. [[Papers/1998-coladan-pinturas-rupestres-oriente#Page 2|Source: PDF pp. 2–6 (printed pp. 661–665)]]

## Studies

### April 1996: Fieldwork

A ten-day regional rock-art campaign photographed the paintings and traced about 10 percent of the known representations. Three of the project’s 17 plastic tracings covered large panels at Espíritu Santo. A surface collection in front of the shelter recovered 12 potsherds and 270 flaked-stone pieces; the ceramic identifications suggested activity in several periods but did not date the paintings. [[Papers/1998-coladan-pinturas-rupestres-oriente#Page 1|Project source: PDF p. 1 (printed p. 660)]] [[Papers/1998-coladan-pinturas-rupestres-oriente#Page 5|Tracings and finds: PDF pp. 5–6 (printed pp. 664–665)]]

### April 1995: Site recording

Elisenda Coladán made a preliminary visit, recognized that the surrounding landscape was likely to contain more painted shelters, and designed the regional recording project carried out the following year. [[Papers/1998-coladan-pinturas-rupestres-oriente#Page 1|Source: PDF p. 1 (printed p. 660)]]

### 1977: Fieldwork

Wolfgang Haberland opened nine test pits. Two pits below the shelter were stopped at shallow depth by large rocks; the others reached about two metres and contained ceramics and obsidian and chert artifacts. Haberland assigned ceramics from one layer to approximately 625–1000 CE and interpreted the deepest stone-tool assemblage as preceramic, but the surviving preliminary report does not document the pits’ precise positions or dimensions. [[Papers/1998-coladan-pinturas-rupestres-oriente#Page 2|Source: PDF p. 2 (printed p. 661)]]

### Mid-January 1976: Site recording

Gloria Elena Hernández and Manuel Roberto López revisited the shelter to assess the condition of its paintings, collect information for continued study, and support a request for national-monument protection. [[Papers/haberland_corinto#Page 6|Source: PDF p. 6 (printed p. 97)]]

### November 1958 and April 1954: Site recording

Haberland visited the cave during archaeological field explorations in 1954 and again in 1958. His preliminary study recorded paintings in red, brown, and yellow, engravings, human and bird figures, cruciform signs, and positive and negative handprints. [[Papers/haberland_corinto#Page 2|Source: PDF pp. 2–5 (printed pp. 93–96)]]
