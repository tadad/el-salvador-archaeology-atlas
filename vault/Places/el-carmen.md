# El Carmen

## Description

El Carmen is an excavated Early Preclassic settlement mound beside an arm of the Estero El Zapote on El Salvador’s western coastal plain. Its compacted-clay floors, possible ovens, storage pits, and refuse deposits indicate domestic activity; radiocarbon samples produced a corrected and calibrated average date of 1590 ± 150 BCE. [[Papers/carmen#Page 3|Setting source: PDF p. 3 (printed p. 1)]] [[Papers/carmen#Page 9|Dating source: PDF p. 9 (printed p. 7)]]

## Studies

### 23 May–17 June 1988: Fieldwork

Researchers excavated 32 square metres in 2 × 2 metre units, generally in arbitrary 25-centimetre levels and by natural layers where features allowed. They identified seven construction stages, three possible high-temperature ovens, 12 storage pits and possibly two more, compacted-clay floors, and refuse deposits. Ceramics and four radiocarbon determinations placed the occupation in the Early Preclassic, while the arrangement of floors, pits, and refuse led the team to interpret the mound as a domestic residence. [[Papers/carmen#Page 3|Source: PDF pp. 3–9 (printed pp. 1–7)]]

### 1986: Site recording

Paul Amaroli identified the mound during archaeological work in the Cara Sucia region. Ceramics exposed in looters’ pits appeared similar to Early Preclassic Ocós-phase material known from Guatemala and Chiapas, prompting the later excavation project. [[Papers/carmen#Page 3|Source: PDF p. 3 (printed p. 1)]]
