# Nancuchiname

## Description

Archaeological evidence from the Hacienda Nancuchiname landscape includes ceramic figurines and low earthen rises. The [[Organizations/ministerio-de-cultura|Ministry of Culture]] identifies four ceramic figurines from Nancuchiname in the Museo Nacional de Antropología collection. Its notices do not describe their forms, chronology, or recovery context. [Ministry notice, 11 April 2024](https://www.cultura.gob.sv/adultos-mayores-de-los-grupos-de-la-direccion-de-integracion-conocieron-el-muna/) [Ministry notice, 24 April 2024](https://www.cultura.gob.sv/grupo-integrando-abuelos-participo-en-maquilishuat-bajo-la-luna/)

A separate collection history records finds from the Joya Verde estate of Hacienda Nancuchiname. It also reports that Wolfgang Haberland surveyed adjoining land in 1953–1954, identified about twelve mounds or small elevations, and recovered one complete ceramic human figure and 61 human heads or fragments. These objects should not be assumed to be the same four figurines now cited by the Ministry. [Museo d’Antichità J. J. Winckelmann, Maya collection history](https://museoantichitawinckelmann.it/visita/collezione-maya/)

## What was found

- Four ceramic figurines in the national museum collection.
- About twelve mounds or low elevations on adjoining land.
- One complete ceramic human figure and 61 ceramic human heads or fragments from the adjoining survey.

## Location and uncertainty

Lardé places the Nancuchiname toponym in Jiquilisco, and modern mapping identifies a broad protected landscape of that name on the eastern side of the lower Lempa. The atlas point uses the mapped protected-area centroid only to make the evidence discoverable; it is not a claimed findspot.
