# Cumbres de Cuscatlán

## Description

A five-sector rescue-archaeology project across the modern Cumbres de Cuscatlán development, adjoining Madre Selva and the former Plan de la Laguna. The atlas point represents the broad project area, not one excavation unit. The project recorded agricultural terraces, domestic platforms, modified slopes, activity areas, offerings, and stone-and-earth buildings. [[Papers/1994-amaroli-hermes-velasquez-antiguo-cuscatlan#Page 3|Source: PDF pp. 3–5 (printed pp. 529–531); project plan on PDF p. 10 (printed p. 536)]] [[Papers/2025-flores-manzano-despues-ilopango#Page 19|Source: PDF pp. 19–28 (printed pp. 29–38), Cumbres rescue synthesis]]

## Studies

### 2024–2025: Archival synthesis

Flores Manzano synthesized the sector reports and unpublished rescue documentation, including the palace, adoratory, offerings, and the very small excavated sample. Sector IV included a two-room building called a palace and a T-shaped platform called an adoratory; offerings there contained Plumbate and Nicoya ceramics and unused obsidian blades, including green Pachuca obsidian. [[Papers/2025-flores-manzano-despues-ilopango#Page 19|Source: 2025-flores-manzano-despues-ilopango.pdf, PDF pp. 19–29]]

### 1997: Reinterpretation

A drain in the Sector IV palace prompted a possible temazcal interpretation, which the 2025 paper reports as disputed. [[Papers/2025-flores-manzano-despues-ilopango#Page 25|Source: 2025-flores-manzano-despues-ilopango.pdf, PDF p. 25 (printed p. 35)]]

### 1993–1994: Fieldwork

Before urban development, the five-sector rescue project began with pedestrian reconnaissance and then used random and directed excavation in small pits and trenches. [[Papers/1994-amaroli-hermes-velasquez-antiguo-cuscatlan#Page 3|Source: 1994-amaroli-hermes-velasquez-antiguo-cuscatlan.pdf, PDF pp. 3–5]] [[Papers/2025-flores-manzano-despues-ilopango#Page 19|Source: 2025-flores-manzano-despues-ilopango.pdf, PDF p. 19]]
