# Madreselva

## Overview

A rescue-excavated site within the Madre Selva development in Antiguo Cuscatlán. The report documents a Late Classic village and two Postclassic groups of structures.

## Chronology

The cited report dates the documented occupations to 600–1524 CE: Late Classic (600–900) and Postclassic (900–1524). Rescue activity is documented in 1992.

## How it was investigated

A development-led rescue program excavated the site. One Postclassic structure was dismantled and reassembled nearby as construction advanced. In July 2024, Flores Manzano revisited the preservation zone and documented a relocated foundation with Polycam and an iPad Pro LiDAR sensor; no new excavation is reported.

## What archaeologists found

Late Classic remains included wattle-and-daub dwellings, refuse pits, and burials near houses, commonly with ceramic vessels. Postclassic excavations recorded a three-structure plaza and a four-mound group; arrowheads lay beside the foundations of burned buildings, but their connection to the Spanish invasion is unproven. The 2024 revisit reported a possible additional mound and a relocated foundation tentatively associated with Group 1.

## Research history

### 2024–2025 — Archival synthesis

A new synthesis reexamined published and unpublished rescue records and presented an explicitly hypothetical reconstruction of the monumental groups.

**Evidence:** [[Papers/2025-flores-manzano-despues-ilopango#Page 1|2025-flores-manzano-despues-ilopango.pdf, PDF pp. 1, 14–29, 40–41]]

### July 2024 — Condition assessment

Flores Manzano revisited the preservation zone, recorded a possible additional mound and relocated foundation, and made a Polycam/iPad LiDAR model; the structural identification is tentative.

**Evidence:** [[Papers/2025-flores-manzano-despues-ilopango#Page 38|2025-flores-manzano-despues-ilopango.pdf, PDF pp. 38–40 (printed pp. 48–50)]]

### 2013 — Collection analysis

A thesis analyzed Copador vessels and their offering context.

**Evidence:** [[Papers/anales-54#Page 10|anales-54.pdf, PDF pp. 10–39]]

### 1998 — Reinterpretation

Madreselva stratigraphy was used to corroborate the position and source of the Cuzcatan tephra.

**Evidence:** [[Papers/milpas#Page 9|milpas.pdf, PDF p. 9]]

### 1992 — Fieldwork

Rescue excavation investigated the site during urban development.

**Evidence:** [[Papers/1994-amaroli-hermes-velasquez-antiguo-cuscatlan#Page 1|1994-amaroli-hermes-velasquez-antiguo-cuscatlan.pdf, PDF pp. 1–8]]

### identidades-18-arqueologia.pdf

The retrospective account dates rescue activity at the Postclassic Madre Selva site to 1992 and reports that one structure, called the Palacio de Madre Selva, was dismantled and reassembled nearby. It does not date the site's Late Classic occupation or provide an excavation coordinate.

### 2025-flores-manzano-despues-ilopango.pdf

**Citation:** PDF pp. 14–18 (printed pp. 24–28), Madre Selva synthesis

[[Papers/2025-flores-manzano-despues-ilopango#Page 14|Open vault paper]] · [Open cited source](https://repositorio.utec.edu.sv/server/api/core/bitstreams/bde7c19d-6250-4ce7-9709-6e4cc3342b21/content#page=14)

The paper synthesizes unpublished 1992 records: about 22 burials, more than 65 vessels, eight Postclassic structures, a Late Classic village of roughly 50 hectares or more, a three-hall elite compound, and a four-mound ceremonial group. Burned structures and arrow points are documented, but the paper does not establish that the burning resulted from the 1524 invasion.
