# Hacienda Tula

## Description

A small archaeological locality on a flat ridge called La Sabana at Hacienda Tula. Three low rises were visible in 1942; two contained pottery-bearing volcanic ash, but the report concluded that their mound-like shape did not reflect constructed architecture. No absolute occupation date was established. Ceramic comparison suggested only that the deposit was roughly contemporaneous with late Tazumal and Campana–San Andrés. Sherds occurred in the lower part of a volcanic-ash layer. [[Papers/longyear#Page 18|Source: PDF p. 18 (printed p. 11), Part II: ‘Tula’]]

## Studies

### March 1942: Fieldwork

Investigators visited the locality and opened one test trench in each of two rises. The site was visited in March 1942. Boggs dug one test trench to sterile soil in each of the two largest rises and compared the recovered pottery with other Salvadoran collections. Sherds occurred in the lower part of a volcanic-ash layer. A fragmentary perforated black-scoria object described as a ‘club head’ was the only stone implement, and no complete vessel was excavated. [[Papers/longyear#Page 18|Source: longyear.pdf, PDF pp. 18, 55–56 (printed pp. 11, 48–49)]]
