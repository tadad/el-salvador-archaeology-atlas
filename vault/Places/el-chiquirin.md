# El Chiquirín

## Overview

A shell midden with stone walls and a burial at Punta El Chiquirín, about 150 metres from the Gulf of Fonseca shore.

## Chronology

Radiocarbon and ceramic studies suggest a Late Classic main occupation, about 600–900 CE. Rescue work ran from late 2002 into early 2003.

## How it was investigated

After an accidental discovery, archaeologists mapped the site and opened six 1 × 1 metre units, later extending the excavation around the burial.

## What archaeologists found

The excavation documented three stone walls filled with shells and a secondary burial. The burial had nine ceramic vessels, red pigment, and volcanic-rock fragments.

## Research history

### 2012 — Collection analysis

A malacological study analyzed shell samples from the rescue excavation.

**Evidence:** asanyamba-concheros-golfo-fonseca.pdf, PDF pp. 24–26

### 2011 — Collection analysis

A comparative ceramic study evaluated the relationship between El Chiquirín and Asanyamba.

**Evidence:** asanyamba-concheros-golfo-fonseca.pdf, PDF pp. 24–26

### 2006–2009 — Fieldwork · Site attribution uncertain

Four seasons mapped and excavated shell middens in the wider Punta Chiquirín locality; the synthesis does not assign every intervention to the 2002 rescue locus.

**Evidence:** asanyamba-concheros-golfo-fonseca.pdf, PDF pp. 24–26

### Late 2002–early 2003 — Fieldwork

A rescue project excavated and recorded the shell midden.

**Evidence:** [[Papers/anales-53#Page 43|anales-53.pdf, PDF pp. 43–61]]

### anales-53.pdf

**Citation:** PDF pp. 44–49 (printed pp. 44–49), rescue excavation and Figures 1–2

[[Papers/anales-53#Page 44|Open vault paper]] · [Open cited source](https://www.cultura.gob.sv/download/revista-anales-53/?wpdmdl=3359#page=44)

The report places the site at Punta Chiquirín about 150 m from the beach and dates the rescue to late 2002 and early 2003. It documents topographic mapping, six initial 1 × 1 m excavation units and later extensions, three stone walls, and a secondary burial with nine ceramic offerings, red pigment, and volcanic-rock fragments. The locality map and excavation plan do not publish a reproducible site coordinate. Ceramic comparison suggests, rather than proves, a Late Classic date of 600–900 CE.
