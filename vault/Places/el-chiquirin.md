# El Chiquirín

## Description

A shell midden with stone walls and a burial at Punta El Chiquirín, about 150 metres from the Gulf of Fonseca shore. Radiocarbon and ceramic studies suggest a Late Classic main occupation, about 600–900 CE. Rescue work ran from late 2002 into early 2003. The excavation documented three stone walls filled with shells and a secondary burial. [[Papers/anales-53#Page 43|Source: PDF p. 43 (printed p. 43), abstract]]

## Studies

### 2012: Collection analysis

A malacological study analyzed shell samples from the rescue excavation. [[Papers/anales-53#Page 43|Source: anales-53.pdf, PDF pp. 43–61]]

### 2011: Collection analysis

A comparative ceramic study evaluated the relationship between El Chiquirín and Asanyamba. [[Papers/anales-53#Page 47|Source: anales-53.pdf, PDF pp. 47–49]]

### 2006–2009: Fieldwork (Site attribution uncertain)

Four seasons mapped and excavated shell middens in the wider Punta Chiquirín locality; the synthesis does not assign every intervention to the 2002 rescue locus. [[Papers/anales-53#Page 45|Source: anales-53.pdf, PDF pp. 45–46]]

### Late 2002–early 2003: Fieldwork

A rescue project excavated and recorded the shell midden. After an accidental discovery, archaeologists mapped the site and opened six 1 × 1 metre units, later extending the excavation around the burial. The excavation documented three stone walls filled with shells and a secondary burial. The burial had nine ceramic vessels, red pigment, and volcanic-rock fragments. [[Papers/anales-53#Page 43|Source: anales-53.pdf, PDF pp. 43–61]]
