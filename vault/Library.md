# Source Collections

## Downloaded locally

- [FUNDAR reference collection](Collections/FUNDAR.md) — 99 downloadable reports,
  manuscripts, maps, artifact studies, and publications centered on Salvadoran
  archaeology.
- [Public-domain primary and early sources](<Collections/Public Domain.md>) — conquest
  letters, colonial reporting, early ethnography, archaeology, mythology, and
  national histories.
- [Institutional sources](Collections/Institutional.md) — 20 downloaded Salvadoran
  university/government publications, Asociación Tikal papers, MUNA journals,
  and open-access scholarly articles, plus records for three files whose direct
  downloads were blocked.
- [Research-lead intake](<Collections/Research Lead Intake.md>) — 34 additional
  open-access archaeology, ethnohistory, language, and oral-history sources
  recovered before retiring the old discovery layer, plus five blocked leads.

Each collection may have a local machine-readable manifest under ignored
`tmp/data/`, with original URL, local path, byte size, and SHA-256 checksum.

The original 127 locally archived PDFs have a [page-addressable searchable
Markdown mirror](Views/Papers.base). The 34 newly reconciled sources are archived
with checksummed provenance and await extraction after the in-progress pipeline
refactor.

## Important linked sources not mirrored

These are strong leads whose available editions, access controls, or rights
status call for linking rather than automatically copying.

### Indigenous-language, folklore, and ethnography

- Leonhard Schultze-Jena, *Indiana II: Mythen in der Muttersprache der Pipil von
  Izalco in El Salvador* (1935). The 1977 Spanish edition is catalogued as
  [*Mitos y leyendas de los Pipiles de Izalco*](https://books.google.com/books?id=hUVrAAAAMAAJ).
  This is a top-priority direct record of Izalco narratives in Nawat/Pipil, but
  editions and translations must be distinguished.
- María de Baratta, *Cuzcatlán típico: ensayo sobre etnofonía de El Salvador*
  (1951), [author catalogue and authorized scans](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obras/autor/baratta-maria-de-85328/).
  Crucial for folklore and ethnomusicology, but also part of mid-century national
  cultural construction.
- Lyle Campbell, *The Pipil Language of El Salvador* (1985). Major linguistic
  source; locate a licensed or library copy for deep work.

### Colonial and administrative sources

- Antonio Gutiérrez y Ulloa, *Estado general de la provincia de San Salvador:
  reyno de Guatemala (año de 1807)*, [1926 edition catalogue and access routes](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/lookupid?key=ha005996150).
- Francisco Antonio de Fuentes y Guzmán, *Recordación Florida*. Public-domain
  editions are available through [Internet Archive search](https://archive.org/search?query=title%3A%28Recordacion+Florida%29),
  but the relevant Salvadoran passages should be identified before mirroring
  multi-volume editions.
- Antonio de Remesal, *Historia general de las Indias occidentales y particular
  de la gobernación de Chiapa y Guatemala*. Trace specific conquest passages and
  identify the best critical/public-domain edition.
- The **Relación de Marroquín** (1548). High-priority archival/critical-edition
  target for population, tribute, and early colonial settlement in San Salvador.

## Source-handling rules

- A local copy preserves access but does not change a work's evidentiary value.
- Record a source's original terminology and publication date when terminology
  differs from current usage.
- Evaluate historical accounts from their authorship, date, purpose, cited
  evidence, and relationship to other sources.
- Record the narrator, collector, language, place, and collection date whenever
  working with oral tradition.
- Cite the exact edition and page; translations can introduce interpretive
  changes.
