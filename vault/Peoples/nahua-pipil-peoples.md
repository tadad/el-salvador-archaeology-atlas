# Nahua and Pipil peoples

This ontology record represents peoples, not an archaeological material category. Place-level identifications require explicit archaeological, historical, linguistic, or ethnohistorical evidence.

## Sources

- [[Papers/hartman-1901-etnografiska-undersokningar-ofver-aztekerna-i-salvador#Page 3|Hartman’s 1901 ethnography, PDF p. 3 and following]] reports fieldwork based in Nahuizalco from 1897 to 1899 and observations in Izalco, Ataco, and other western communities. Its historical terminology and generalizations require critical reading.
- [[Papers/jstor-534659#Page 3|Hartman’s 1907 mythology article, PDF pp. 3–6]] presents stories collected during more than a year of fieldwork among people he called Aztecs or Pipiles, including material located at Ahuachapán and Nahuizalco.
- [[Papers/marroquin_indigena#Page 759|Marroquín’s 1975 survey, printed pp. 759–760]] discusses Izalco, Nahuizalco, and Juayúa as communities where adult speakers still used Nawat. Its racialized categories, population estimates, and policy framing are historical evidence, not present-day classifications.
- [[Papers/vivo_escoto_poblamiento#Page 12|Vivo Escoto’s 1973 migration study, PDF pp. 12–13 and 27–28]] links Salvadoran place names and archaeological localities to proposed Nahua migrations. Its migration chronology is an interpretation that should not be treated as established merely because it connects names.
