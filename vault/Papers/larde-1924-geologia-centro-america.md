## Notes

### Source edition and OCR

This is the complete work as reprinted in *Anales del Museo Nacional David J. Guzmán*, tomo IV, no. 14 (September 1953). The local derivative contains source-PDF pages 10–54, renumbered here as PDF pages 1–45. Fresh 300-DPI Spanish-and-English OCR was used throughout; it is searchable but the two-column journal layout still causes occasional reading-order and character errors.

### Research leads

- Lardé critically examined the story that eruptions formed Lake Güija and submerged Azacualpa and Güijar. He instead associated the reported ruins near [[Places/belen-guijat-uixaca-mazahua|Belén-Güijat]] with colonial-period Mazahua-Uixaca and argued that the lake was much older. Both the site identification and geological conclusion need modern verification. [[#Page 11|PDF pp. 11–12]]
- The San Salvador–Ilopango discussion records stratified volcanic ash, pumice, a dark archaeological soil, and buried objects. It is an early attempt to connect eruption sequences with archaeological deposits, although its dates and claimed human–megafauna association are obsolete and should not be accepted. [[#Page 22|PDF pp. 22–24]]
- The paper briefly locates [[Places/tecpa-zapotitan|Tecpa]] west of Sitio del Niño and ties its inundation to Río Sucio and the Zapotitán basin, providing an independent version of the longer argument in *El Volcán de Izalco*. [[#Page 20|PDF p. 20]]

### Limitations

This is historically important geology, not a current stratigraphic framework. Its geological ages, eruption dates, correlations, and archaeological chronology require comparison with modern volcanology and radiometric dating.
