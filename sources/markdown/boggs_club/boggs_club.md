ARCHAEOLOGICAL MATERIAL FROM THE CLUB INTERNACIONAL

In 1939, during construction of the present building of
the Club Internacional in the center of the city of San Salva-
dor, workmen excavating the basement discovered a deposit of
prehistoric objects associated with a stratum of volcanic ash,
This material, according to Sr. Don Jose Maria Duran, architect
and building contractor in charge of the work, was found mixed
together in a very small area. No burials or structures of
pre-Columbian age have been reported within at least 1 km, of
the finds.

Many visitors to San Salvador have viewed some of the
specimens discussed in the: present Note, and several of the
polychrome bowls have been commented upon by Longyear and
others, The following account is the first, I believe, to |
attempt a fairly detailed annotation of all the finds together
with their place of discovery. ‘The importance of the Club col-
lection arises from the facts that one specific stratum of vol-
canic ash contained all the specimens, and that a number of
easily recognizable, possibly datable, pottery. wares were rep-

The area of San Salvador has been blanketed periodically
in the past by successive falls of white volcanic ash, and
various collections of prehistoric objects have been recovered
from what is apparently the most recent of these strata, Cer-
tain materials were undoubtedly intrustve-and refer to a later
date than the ash fell (see Boggs, "A Preconquest Tomb on. the.
Cerro del Zapote, El Salvador," Note 32). -Others, such as
those now being considered, must refer to the time of the fall

itself,

Inasmuch as postconquest alterations of the leteat ash
layer by man, ‘animals, erosion, and the like invite confusion
in determining stratigraphical position of finds. said to be. :
from that level, it is best to consider only those prende:

known, or believed,  to be undisturbed.

Nonintrusive, undisturbed deposits are characterized by
lack of association with mounds or burials, by the facts that
many of the objects are fragmentary and that no apparent: care
Was taken in arranging them. Those known=to me ceeurred:near
the base of the latest ash, whereas intrusive deposits were
found at almost any elevation in. that-stratan,:-It-does not
seen likely that such nonintrusive deposits. represent formal
caches. Many individual specimens from different finds of this
category are closely comparable to one another, and the combi-
nations of types within such groupings of objects are suffi-
ciently similar that one may believe their deposition to have

occurred at approximately the same time.

Obviously, the importance of relating easily identifiable
pottery types to distinctive strata of- volcanic ash derives
from the possibility of dating both pottery and ash; so that
whenever, in other parts of the geographical zone investigated,
previously unidentified and undated human remains are brought
to light from the same ash layers, or accompanying the known,
Gated pottery, some chronology at least may be set up. Up to
the present time, no thorough investigation has been conducted
of the extent or composition of any ash layer in El Salvador,
although recent studies by the Salvadoran Instituto Teenologico
emphasize the value of such research to archaeologists,
